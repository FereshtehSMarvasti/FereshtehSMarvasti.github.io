What this twin models — and its limits +

The 3D geometry is adapted from the supplied critical-management-3d.html. The interactive lab replaces its scheduled equipment states with a deterministic thermal, power-balance and protection model. The guided replay retains the original cinematic story separately.

Two 40 MW transformer equivalents and a 30 MW reserve supply a shared bus. Demand is allocated across available paths; unmet demand accumulates as unserved energy. This is a capacity model, not an AC power-flow solver: voltage, frequency, reactive power, transient stability and vendor protection curves are not simulated.

Temperature follows dT/dt = (T_equilibrium − T) / τ; the illustrative equilibrium depends on ambient temperature, squared loading, cooling availability and fire. T1 has a 120-second sustained overload timer at 110% and a 115 °C thermal trip; T2 trips at 110 °C after cooling failure. These are demonstration parameters, not operational settings.

Physical state and CMS observations are separate: a camera can report smoke while the model is healthy, and switching can be blocked by missing feedback. Evidence sufficiency is a rule-based checklist, not a calibrated probability. Approvals are interface interactions, not authenticated operational authorisations.

Simulation-backed digital twin prototype. It is not connected to a physical substation. A field deployment would need asset-specific parameters, telemetry synchronisation, validated models and integration with the existing protection and control systems.
